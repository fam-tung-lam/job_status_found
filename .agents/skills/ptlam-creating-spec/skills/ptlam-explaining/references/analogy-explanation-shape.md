# The Analogy Explanation Shape

This file covers the four parts of an analogy explanation, their order, and the
Markdown a direct answer uses. A calling skill may render the parts differently
but must keep their meaning and order.

Lock the chosen domain and use it throughout. Match words and depth to the
learner. Be vivid without losing precision.

## 1. In a sentence

Reuse the one-sentence literal answer unchanged, as a blockquote:

> [One-sentence literal summary]

## 2. The map

Translate each essential story element into exactly one real concept:

| In the story      | In [concept name] | Why it maps                          |
| ----------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| [analogy element] | [literal element] | [preserved relationship or behavior] |

## 3. The story

Tell a short story entirely inside the analogy domain, in second person. Never
name the literal concept inside the story. Use an ordered list when order or
cause matters; otherwise short prose or bullets.

## 4. Where it breaks

Name every material way the analogy leaves out, oversimplifies, or misrepresents
the literal concept. One or two limits are usually enough. If the analogy needs
many caveats, it is the wrong analogy: reject it and pick a stronger candidate.

Finish when all four parts are present, fit the learner, and state every
material limit. Add no extra introduction or conclusion.
